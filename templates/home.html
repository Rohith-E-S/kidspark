Three steps

One sitting, then a plan you can start on Monday.

  1. Step 1

    Add the child

    Name, age and grade. Age picks the question difficulty band and the tone of the recommendations — a 7-year-old and a 15-year-old get different homework.

  2. Step 2

    Take the 24 questions

    Big tappable cards with emoji hints. Part 1 is puzzles, Part 2 is preferences. The child can pause halfway and resume later — nothing is lost.

  3. Step 3

    Read the report together

    A radar of cognitive strengths, a bar chart of interests, a named persona, and four concrete activities plus four future pathways. Printable for school meetings.

Part 1

Cognitive aptitude

Twelve questions with one right answer, each carrying a difficulty weight from 1 to 3. Scores are normalised to 0–100 per dimension, so hard questions are worth more without making the result unreadable.

  • Logical & Mathematical

    Patterns, number puzzles, deductions.

  • Verbal & Linguistic

    Vocabulary, comprehension, word links.

  • Spatial & Visual

    Mental rotation, shape matching, memory.

  • Critical & Analytical

    Cause and effect, everyday reasoning.

Part 2

Areas of interest

Twelve scenarios with no wrong answer. Each option carries an affinity weight — love it, sometimes, or not for me — which is what turns a child's enjoyment into a usable preference score.

  • STEM & Discovery

    Experiments, coding, how things work.

  • Arts & Creative Expression

    Drawing, music, storytelling, design.

  • Nature & Outdoors

    Animals, environment, space, exploring.

  • Social & Leadership

    Teamwork, helping, organising, talking.

Every strong 8-point answer becomes a strength worth naming.

Sixteen persona combinations, each with its own reading, its own weekly activities and its own future pathways. Start with a name and an age.
